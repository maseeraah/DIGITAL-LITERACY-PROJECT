Case Study: Phishing Attack on a College Student
Phishing is a cybercrime where attackers trick individuals into revealing sensitive information such as passwords, banking details, or personal data by posing as a trusted entity. It typically happens through fraudulent emails, text messages, or fake websites that closely resemble legitimate ones.

Step-by-step process of the crime:

The attacker creates a fake email that looks like it is from a trusted source (e.g., a bank or university).

The email contains urgent language, such as “Your account will be blocked unless you verify now.”

A link in the email directs the victim to a counterfeit website designed to capture login credentials.

Once the victim enters their details, the attacker gains access to their real account.

The stolen information is then used for financial theft, identity fraud, or selling data on the dark web.

Typical targets:  
Students and young professionals are often targeted because they may lack awareness of cybersecurity practices. Phishing emails often exploit their reliance on online banking, e-learning platforms, and digital payment apps.

Consequences:  
Victims may lose money directly from their accounts, face identity theft, or suffer reputational damage if their accounts are misused. In one real incident reported by The Hindu BusinessLine (2023), several Indian students lost money after responding to fake scholarship emails that redirected them to fraudulent payment portals.

This case highlights the importance of verifying links, enabling multi-factor authentication, and being cautious of unsolicited messages.
